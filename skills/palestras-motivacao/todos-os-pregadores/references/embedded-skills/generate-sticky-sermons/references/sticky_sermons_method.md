# Sticky Sermons Method

Use this reference when the user wants a sermon built around the Sticky Sermons methodology by Brandon Kelley and Joe Hoagland, with double exegesis, Gospel Gold, one Bottom Line, and the six-movement sermon arc.

## Central Principle

One sermon. One idea. One action.

If the message cannot be reduced to one memorable statement, preparation is incomplete.

## Five Mandatory Ingredients

Every sermon must:

1. solve a real problem
2. be true to Scripture
3. be useful for ordinary life
4. stay focused on one central idea
5. drive toward action

## Double Exegesis

### Exegesis of the text

- identify historical, literary, and cultural context
- determine what the text meant to the original audience
- distinguish what the text says from what the preacher wants it to say
- find the central biblical truth

### Exegesis of the people

- identify the listener's real pain or tension
- identify internal narratives about self, God, and life
- identify what they think they need versus what they really need
- identify likely resistances and hidden questions

The tension in the sermon must match the listener's actual tension.

## Gospel Gold

Every sermon must bring the Gospel to the surface, regardless of text or topic.

Use the `Lie / Longing / Light` framework:

- Lie: what false belief is the text exposing?
- Longing: what deep human desire is underneath the struggle?
- Light: how do the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus confront the lie and satisfy the longing?

The Gospel Gold should appear naturally in Truth or in the transition from Truth to Application.

## Bottom Line

The Bottom Line is the anchor sentence of the sermon.

It should be:

- short
- declarative
- memorable
- central to the text
- strong enough to repeat and carry through the week

Use it this way:

1. determine it before writing
2. introduce it in Application
3. repeat it two or three times
4. end on it or near it

## Six Movements

### 1. Engage

Gain attention in the first 60 seconds.

Possible openings:

- story
- provocative question
- unexpected fact
- strong quote
- recognizable daily-life scene

Never open with the text, series framing, announcements, or apology language.

### 2. Tension

Name the gap between where the listener is and where they need or want to be.

Tension may be:

- pain
- confusion
- contradiction
- aspiration

Do not resolve it yet.

### 3. Truth

Show how Scripture answers the tension.

Steps:

1. contextualize the text
2. teach what it says
3. show what it meant
4. let the text control the sermon

### 4. Application

Bridge from then to now.

Mental model:

`Jesus said this -> this meant that -> this means this for you today.`

This is where the Bottom Line appears for the first time.

### 5. Inspiration or Reflection

Choose one:

- Inspiration: help the listener imagine a changed future
- Reflection: help the listener examine the present honestly

Use questions, not just more assertions.

### 6. Action

Call the listener to one concrete next step this week.

Rules:

- one main action
- specific
- doable this week
- spiritually connected to the text and Gospel

At most two calls are allowed only when one is for the nonbeliever and one is for the believer.

## Writing and Delivery Principles

- use a conversational voice
- maintain honesty and real-life fragility
- prefer simple phrasing to ornate phrasing
- use illustrations to illuminate truth, not replace it
- build the sermon like a narrative arc, not a list dump

## Extension Beyond Sunday

The sermon should continue through the week:

- small group questions from Tension and Bottom Line
- social media graphic based on the Bottom Line
- short clip built from the clearest sermon section
- weekly follow-up tied to the Action

## Default Deliverable

Unless the user requests a different format, produce:

1. Pre-sermon declaration with the five required statements.
2. Complete sermon manuscript.
3. One clear action step.
4. A formatted `.docx` file that passes the minimum word count threshold.

## Word Document Requirement

- The standard output is a Microsoft Word `.docx` document.
- For a complete sermon, enforce at least 7,500 words unless the user explicitly changes the requirement.
- Build the manuscript first, then convert it to `.docx`.
- Confirm the final word count before treating the job as complete.
