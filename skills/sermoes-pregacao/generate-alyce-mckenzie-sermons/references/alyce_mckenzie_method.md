# Alyce McKenzie Scenic Preaching Method

Use this file as the governing reference when generating sermons in Alyce McKenzie's style.

## Core Conviction

Scene is the new story. Contemporary hearers often process life visually and scenically before they process it propositionally. Therefore a sermon can invite them into truth through scenes they can inhabit.

This does not replace theology. It changes the mode through which theology is experienced and remembered.

## The Three Scapes

### Inscape

The preacher's interior life:

- memories
- fears
- hopes
- grace moments
- wounds

Use the inscape honestly, but never as self-display. It must serve the hearer.

### Landscape

The world of the congregation and community:

- family realities
- local pressures
- public events
- cultural assumptions
- weekly lived scenes

The sermon must be made for these people, now.

### Textscape

The biblical world as a scene:

- characters
- space
- conflict
- sensory detail
- timing
- movement

Read the text scenically before moving into formal commentary.

## The Knack for Noticing

The preacher as wise observer notices scenes others miss.

Throughout the week, collect:

- gestures
- fragments of dialogue
- recurring images
- physical spaces
- tensions in ordinary life

This noticing is the raw material of scenic preaching.

## The Four Elements of a Live Scene

### Pulse

Something is at stake. The hearer feels tension rather than merely hears about it.

### Purpose

The scene carries theological meaning without needing to explain itself immediately.

### Plot

Something changes between the start and the end of the scene. Without change, there is only a tableau.

### Point of View

The hearer must know from whose angle the scene is seen. Point of view is a theological choice because it determines where empathy and discovery enter.

## Scenic Reading of Scripture

Before formal exegesis, ask:

- Who is here?
- Where are they?
- What is at stake?
- What is the telling detail?
- What changes?

Then let historical and literary study deepen the scene rather than replace it.

## Doctrine as Stage Direction

Doctrine should shape how the scene is built, interpreted, and illuminated.

Avoid pausing the sermon to float doctrine above the scene as detached theory. Let doctrine work the way stage direction works in drama: it guides the action and reveals meaning from within.

## Structural Forms

### Single Expanded Scene

One scene carries the sermon almost entirely.

### Multiple Scenes in Dialogue

Two or more scenes illuminate one another across the sermon.

### Opening and Closing Scene

The sermon opens a scene, develops the biblical and theological material, then returns to the same or analogous scene transformed at the end.

## Ending

The sermon should equip hearers for the scenes of the coming week.

Do not end in abstraction alone. End in a transformed scene that sends the hearer toward greater:

- kindness
- justice
- courage

## Final Diagnostic

Before finishing, verify:

- the hearer is inside scenes, not merely hearing descriptions
- the three scapes genuinely cross
- scenes are not overexplained
- characters feel real, not symbolic only
- the ending sends the hearer back to life with a transformed way of inhabiting the next scene
