# John Stott Expository Preaching Method

Use this file as the governing reference when generating sermons in John Stott's style.

## Core Conviction

True Christian preaching is expository preaching. The preacher stands between two worlds:

- the world of the biblical text
- the world of contemporary hearers

The sermon must build a bridge between them. If it remains only in the biblical world, it becomes historically accurate but pastorally distant. If it remains only in the modern world, it becomes relevant talk without biblical authority.

## The Dominant Thought

Every sermon must be distilled into one dominant thought. If the preacher needs two sentences, the thought has not yet been clarified.

### Requirements

- one sentence
- clear enough to be spoken aloud
- specific to this text
- relevant to these hearers
- repeated several times across the sermon

## The Exegetical Idea and the Dominant Thought

- The exegetical idea states what the biblical author said to the original hearers.
- The dominant thought states what that truth means now for this congregation.

The sermon should make the difference explicit before the body begins.

## The Five Portraits of the Preacher

### Steward

- The preacher dispenses what was entrusted, not personal favorites.
- Vice to avoid: substituting creativity for fidelity.

### Herald

- The preacher proclaims a received message with derivative authority.
- Vice to avoid: softening what the text clearly says.

### Witness

- The preacher speaks from what has first passed through personal experience.
- Vice to avoid: secondhand preaching.

### Father

- The preacher knows the people and applies the text with pastoral empathy.
- Vice to avoid: abstract preaching without faces.

### Servant

- The preacher serves Christ and the hearers, not ego or reputation.
- Vice to avoid: using the pulpit to impress.

## Double Listening

The sermon must show serious attention to two worlds.

### Listen to the Text

- grammar
- structure
- context
- genre
- theological place in Scripture

### Listen to the World

- pressures of the congregation
- current assumptions and cultural narratives
- fears, doubts, griefs, and temptations of real hearers

## Silent Dialogue

Stott prepares by anticipating the listener's internal objections. For each major point, the preacher should answer at least these questions:

1. Why does this matter?
2. Is this really true?
3. It may be true generally, but what about my case?

The sermon should answer those objections before the listener says them aloud.

## Suggested Sermon Movement

### 1. Introduction

- awaken curiosity
- sharpen appetite
- move hearers toward the need the text addresses
- do not reveal everything too soon

### 2. Exposition of the Text

- explain the text in context
- uncover its structure and force
- show the argument of the biblical author

### 3. Bridge Building and Silent Dialogue

- move toward present experience
- voice likely objections
- answer them with the text's logic and truth

### 4. Contemporary Application

- address concrete lives and situations
- show what the text means for this congregation now
- keep application specific and pastorally aware

### 5. Conclusion with Appeal

- do more than summarize
- press toward a verdict of the will
- call for a specific response grounded in the text

## Tone

- clear
- serious
- humble
- pastorally warm
- intellectually honest

Avoid platform theatrics, vague applications, or sermon structures that overpower the text.

## Final Diagnostic

Before finishing, verify:

- the dominant thought controls the whole sermon
- the structure emerged from the text
- both worlds have been studied and connected
- objections have been answered
- the conclusion makes a real appeal
- the authority of the sermon comes from Scripture, not from the preacher
