# John MacArthur Expository Preaching Method

Use this file as the governing reference when generating sermons in John MacArthur's style.

## Core Conviction

The preacher does not invent the sermon. He discovers what the text says.

This depends on two non-negotiables:

- the inerrancy of Scripture
- the sufficiency of Scripture

Therefore the text must determine:

- the content
- the structure
- the doctrinal claims
- the moral demand

## Expository Logic

### 1. Delimit the Text

Choose a coherent literary unit, not an arbitrary fragment.

### 2. Discover Original Intent

State what the human author, under the Spirit's inspiration, meant to communicate to the original audience.

### 3. Form the Dominant Idea

Reduce the passage to one complete controlling idea.

### 4. Expose Verse by Verse

Follow the text's own sequence and argument. Do not impose an external outline.

### 5. Explain Decisive Words

Use Greek or Hebrew only when it materially affects meaning.

### 6. Let Scripture Interpret Scripture

Cross-reference carefully so the passage is illuminated by the Bible's own unity.

### 7. State Doctrine and Implications

Move from explanation to implication. Do not leap too quickly into practical scenarios.

### 8. Exhort with Gravitas

Where the text commands, warn, comforts, or summons, the preacher must do the same with seriousness.

## Explanation, Implication, Application

MacArthur-style preaching distinguishes these:

- explanation: what the text says
- implication: what necessarily follows theologically
- application: the Spirit's specific use of those implications in individual lives

The sermon may exhort clearly, but it should resist invented practical case studies that are not naturally anchored in the text.

## Language and Tone

- serious
- text-governed
- doctrinally clear
- confident in biblical authority
- not decorative

Avoid:

- ornamental openings
- storytelling that competes with the text
- sentimental summaries
- softened warnings where the text is strong

## Theological Pillars Often Relevant

1. Inerrancy and authority of Scripture
2. Sovereignty of God and Reformed soteriology
3. Lordship salvation
4. Cessationism
5. Dispensational distinctives

Not every passage foregrounds all five, but the sermon should know which ones are in play.

## Final Diagnostic

Before finishing, verify:

- the sermon emerged from the passage
- the dominant idea is text-born
- lexical work is necessary and useful
- doctrine is explicit
- implications are weighty
- exhortation is faithful to the text's own force
