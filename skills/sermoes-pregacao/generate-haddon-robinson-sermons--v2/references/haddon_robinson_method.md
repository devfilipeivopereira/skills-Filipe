# Haddon Robinson Sermon Method

Use this reference when the user wants a sermon built around Haddon W. Robinson's expository preaching method from `Biblical Preaching`, with one Big Idea and a passage-derived expository structure.

## Operational Definition

Expository preaching is the communication of one biblical concept derived from historical, grammatical, and literary study of a passage in context, first applied to the preacher, and then through the preacher applied to the listeners.

This definition requires:

1. one concept
2. study-based derivation
3. prior application to the preacher
4. truth through personality

## Foundational Principle

Preaching is truth through personality.

The preacher must be changed by the text before trying to change others with it.

## One Big Idea

The sermon should function like a bullet, not a load of scattered shot.

Multiple unrelated points do not make a sermon. All parts must serve one controlling idea.

## Ten Stages

### 1. Select the passage

- use a complete thought unit
- avoid fragmenting the author's flow

### 2. Study the passage

- wrestle with the text before consulting commentaries
- note context, genre, grammar, repetition, and argument flow
- ask:
  - what does the text say?
  - what does it mean?
  - where does it meet me?

### 3. Form the Exegetical Idea

Use subject plus complement.

- Subject: what is the author talking about?
- Complement: what is the author saying about that subject?

The Exegetical Idea states what the biblical author said to the original audience.

### 4. Test the Exegetical Idea

Ask:

- what does this mean?
- is it true?
- so what?

### 5. Form the Homiletical Idea

Translate the Exegetical Idea into contemporary present-tense language for today's listeners.

The Homiletical Idea should be:

- one sentence
- oral and memorable
- specific and relevant

### 6. Define purpose and outcomes

The idea is what the sermon says.
The purpose is what the sermon does.

State the purpose clearly and define expected outcomes in:

- cognitive
- affective
- volitional

### 7. Use the questions and IDEA

Every listener asks:

1. what does this mean?
2. is this true?
3. so what?

The preacher should use IDEA flexibly:

- Illustrate
- Defend
- Explain
- Apply

Do not overstay in explanation only.

### 8. Choose the structure

Possible structures:

- deductive
- inductive
- semi-inductive

Choose the structure that best serves the Big Idea and the audience.

### 9. Fill the outline

Use the ladder of abstraction:

- move from abstract truth to concrete experience
- use illustrations that descend into real life

Always connect imperatives to Gospel indicatives.

### 10. Craft introduction and conclusion

Introduction should:

- capture attention
- create need
- introduce the subject

Conclusion should:

- introduce no new material
- drive a verdict
- avoid vague phrases like `live in light of these truths`

## Governing Principles

- the audience hears a person, not just a sermon
- the Big Idea governs all points
- the purpose must be specific
- the sermon should be concrete whenever possible
- authority comes from fidelity to the text and evidence of transformed personality
