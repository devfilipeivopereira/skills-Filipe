# Steven Furtick Sermon Method

Use this reference when the user wants a sermon built around Steven Furtick's authentic style, with strong title wordplay, autobiographical opening, perspective shift, congregational participation, and proclamation-heavy climax.

## Core Identity

Steven Furtick is a narrative preacher who uses personal story, perspective inversion, and congregational activation to make biblical truth feel immediate.

His style combines:

- casual self-aware humor
- emotional escalation
- congregation participation
- phrase repetition
- perspective reframe
- biblical dramatization

## Title Wordplay

This is the most recognizable technique.

Take a familiar phrase from the text or common Christian language and invert, replace, or reposition one word to create the sermon's title and recurring phrase.

Rules:

- it should emerge from the text
- it should feel like discovery
- it should usually stay under five words
- it should be easy to repeat

## Opening Story

Open with a recent, real, sensory story from the preacher's life.

Requirements:

- recent event
- real names or details when fitting
- ordinary setting
- light humor or self-awareness
- pivot into the sermon tension

## Congregation Participation

Use multiple participation moments through the sermon.

Examples:

- `[TOQUE O SEU VIZINHO E DIGA]`
- `[ALGUEM GRITE]`
- `[DIGA DE NOVO]`
- `[PAUSA]`

Do not cluster too many in a row. Interleave them with narrative and teaching.

## Three Speeds

### 1. Conversation

- opening stories
- humor
- casual narration

### 2. Teaching

- biblical explanation
- contextual clarity
- careful observation

### 3. Proclamation

- repetition
- short declarations
- increased intensity
- maximum participation

The sermon should move between these speeds rather than staying flat.

## Perspective Reframe

This is the sermon's theological core.

Common shape:

- you call it `X`
- God calls it `Y`

The listener's interpretation is reframed by the text.

## Biblical Character Dramatization

Narrate the character in present-tense feel when appropriate.

Requirements:

- stay faithful to the text
- verbalize likely internal struggle carefully
- connect the character's tension to the listener's tension

## Language of the Enemy

Use the enemy motif when naming lies, intimidation, or internal sabotage, but always move toward exposure and victory rather than fear.

## Series Awareness

The sermon may belong to a broader series with its own branded name.

Mention that naturally in the opening when useful.

## Full Flow

### Opening

- personal story
- sensory detail
- humor
- move into tension

### Title Reveal

- build expectation
- reveal the wordplay
- have the congregation repeat it

### Context

- brief context
- one unexpected text detail

### Dramatization

- narrate the biblical scene
- bring the listener inside it

### Reframe

- present the divine reinterpretation
- repeat the mantra
- increase intensity

### Climax

- proclamation mode
- repeated phrase
- strong congregation participation

### Ending

- slow down
- deliver practical challenge
- repeat the mantra more quietly
- move into prayer or response
