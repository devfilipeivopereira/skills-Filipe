# Louie Giglio Preaching Method

Use this file as the governing reference when generating sermons in Louie Giglio's style.

## Core Conviction

Preaching is an act of worship that calls hearers into worship. The sermon should make God greater in the hearer's sight, shrink the giant that has occupied the field of vision, and bring the hearer into encounter with Jesus.

The movement is from:

- the cosmos to the heart
- greatness to nearness
- awe to surrender

## The Three Theological Anchors

### 1. The Glory of God

Every sermon needs a moment of expansion where God's greatness becomes visceral.

This may come through:

- the cosmos
- creation
- science
- an image of scale
- a sharp cultural contradiction

### 2. Radical Grace

Grace is for the hearer in the breaking point, not only for the strong version of the hearer.

Therefore the sermon should create room for vulnerability before proclaiming rescue.

### 3. Extravagant Worship

The sermon should end in worship as the natural response to what has been revealed about God.

## The Giant

A giant is anything that looms larger in the hearer's sight than God.

Common examples:

- fear
- rejection
- comfort
- anger
- addiction

The sermon must name the giant specifically, pastorally, and without moralistic scolding.

## Controlling Question for the Text

When entering the biblical text, ask:

- what does this passage reveal about who God is?

Do not begin by asking first:

- what am I supposed to do?

The text becomes a window into God's character, and only then a source of transformed living.

## The Indescribable Moment

At some point the sermon should create an "indescribable" moment in which God's greatness becomes vivid enough that the hearer cannot dismiss it as abstraction.

This may come through:

- the naming of stars
- the scale of creation
- a biological image
- a natural wonder
- a biblical image of majesty

## Personal Narrative

Use the preacher's story as evidence, not as heroic triumph.

### Rules

- start at the breaking point
- show weakness before the turn
- make God the rescuer, not the preacher the hero

## Eight Phase Flow

1. Establish the larger world
2. Diagnose the giant
3. Enter the text with God as the greatest character
4. Connect greatness to the hearer's actual giant
5. Offer personal narrative as evidence
6. State the resolution clearly
7. Transition into worship
8. Offer invitation and commitment

## Ending

Do not end with self-improvement language.

End with:

- worship
- surrender
- Jesus made greater
- a clear invitation for both unbeliever and drifting believer

## Final Diagnostic

Before finishing, verify:

- God is greater in the hearer's sight than at the beginning
- the giant has been named and relativized by God's presence
- the text has revealed God's character
- the sermon moves naturally into worship
- Jesus is the center and fame is directed to him, not to the preacher
