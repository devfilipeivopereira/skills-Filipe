# Fred Craddock Sermon Method

Use this reference when the user wants a sermon built around Fred B. Craddock's inductive method, the New Homiletic, or a sermon structured as discovery rather than deduction.

## The Problem It Solves

Traditional deductive preaching often begins at the finish line and leaves the listener with only acceptance or rejection. Craddock's method instead invites the listener into a shared journey of discovery.

## Central Principle

The sermon moves from concrete particulars toward Gospel truth:

- particular
- particular
- particular
- Gospel insight

Or in listener experience:

- recognition
- complication
- surprise
- new hearing

## Three Movements

### 1. Knot

Start where the listener already is.

Requirements:

- open with recognizable human experience
- use language of recognition and familiarity
- build trust and shared ground
- shuttle between the text's world and the listener's world
- do not yet announce the destination

### 2. Shock

Introduce the genuine complication that the Gospel requires.

Requirements:

- use dialectical turns such as `yes, and yet`
- show that the listener's prior assumption is incomplete
- let the tension be felt, not merely explained
- keep shuttling between biblical world and present world
- deepen the same insight in spiral form
- do not solve the tension too early

### 3. New Hearing

Allow the listener to arrive at a reoriented hearing of the Gospel.

Requirements:

- let the truth emerge rather than be packaged
- reveal the sermon sentence only when it has been earned, if stated at all
- keep the ending open enough for inward completion
- trust the Spirit's work in what remains unstated

## Three Instruments

### Shuttle

Move repeatedly between the world of the text and the world of the congregation so each interprets the other.

### Spiral

Return to the central insight again and again from new angles and at greater depth.

### Shock

Use real complication to unsettle familiar assumptions so the Gospel can be heard freshly.

## Governing Principles

- start where the listener is
- let the listener arrive at the conclusion
- subordinate structure to movement
- say one thing in many ways
- locate authority between the listener and the Book
- treat the sermon as a journey
- leave room for the Spirit in the open ending

## Preparation Pattern

1. read the text inductively
2. distill the sermon sentence
3. collect material
4. eliminate anything that does not serve the sentence
5. arrange material backwards from the ending if needed
6. map Knot, Shock, and New Hearing for this congregation
7. plan the shuttle between biblical world and contemporary world
8. decide how open the ending should remain

## What Not To Do

- do not announce the conclusion in the opening
- do not default to three deductive points
- do not crowd the sermon with multiple ideas
- do not ask multiple central questions
- do not close every loop at the end
- do not use the method as an excuse for weak preparation

## Default Deliverable

Unless the user requests a different format, produce:

1. Pre-sermon declaration with the five required statements.
2. Complete sermon manuscript.
3. One open but clear final hearing of the Gospel.
4. A formatted `.docx` file that passes the minimum word count threshold.

## Word Document Requirement

- The standard output is a Microsoft Word `.docx` document.
- For a complete sermon, enforce at least 7,500 words unless the user explicitly changes the requirement.
- Build the manuscript first, then convert it to `.docx`.
- Confirm the final word count before treating the job as complete.
