# Brian Jones Sermon Method

Use this reference when the user wants a sermon built around Brian Jones's preaching method, CCV-style conversion preaching, story-led sermon construction, or the Introduction-Explanation-Application flow.

## Core Philosophy

The method is built for conversion growth, not insider church language.

Two governing principles:

1. Story is the delivery system. Open with a strong story, close with a strong story, and use two more strong stories in the middle.
2. The sermon is designed primarily for an unchurched listener, not for a mature Christian seeking maximum technical depth.

## Listener First

Before outlining the sermon, define the target listener. Use a concrete `Unchurched John` profile:

- adult
- intelligent
- skeptical of religion
- spiritually open but not fluent in church language
- dealing with ordinary life pressures

Also define how this listener likely feels on arrival:

- distracted
- guarded
- emotionally occupied
- curious but not yet engaged

## Preparation Discipline

Jones's discipline has two phases:

1. Deep study for roughly 6 to 10 hours.
2. A two-minute sprint where the preacher writes the real outline under pressure.

The sermon should reflect that distilled clarity. Avoid analysis paralysis.

## Action Statement

Before writing the sermon, define the action statement.

The action statement must be:

- imperative
- specific
- verifiable
- time-bound or time-implied

Examples:

- weak: `Be more generous.`
- strong: `Give the first 10 percent of your next paycheck before you spend anything else.`

## Sermon Structure

### Act 1: Introduction

Goal: create need and emotional tension.

Never:

- open by reading the text
- open by asking people to open the Bible
- open with historical context
- announce the theme too early

Build the introduction in four beats:

1. Jab 1: strong opening story or stimulus
2. Jab 2: second stimulus that widens relevance
3. Jab 3: personal urgency and core burden
4. Right Hook: invite people to the text only now

### Act 2: Explanation

Goal: show what the text really says and why it matters.

Sequence:

1. Present the biblical text.
2. Signal that you will surface what the listener would not notice alone.
3. Extract no more than three core truths.

Use history, original languages, or cross-references only when they sharpen the listener's understanding and move the sermon toward the action statement.

### Act 3: Application

Goal: move people to obedience, not mere agreement.

Required sequence:

1. state the action directly
2. show personal honesty
3. give specific replicable examples
4. name objections
5. add transformation stories, questions, and pressure lines
6. call for action without ambiguity
7. close with a concrete response and strongest final story

## Gold Rules

- one sermon
- one idea
- one action
- keep the structure simple so the listener can spend energy on substance
- put the listener before the text in preparation order
- use stories as method, not decoration
- keep the sermon edited hard
- if something does not serve the action statement, cut it

## Pre-Writing Checklist

- Is the target listener specific?
- Is the arrival-state clear?
- Did the sermon emerge from a distilled outline rather than endless expansion?
- Is the action statement specific and testable?
- Does Jab 1 create tension without solving it?
- Is the Right Hook brief?
- Are there three truths or fewer in Explanation?
- Does Application contain real-life examples and real transformation stories?
- Is there a direct call to action before the ending?
- Does the sermon finish with the best story?

## Default Deliverable

Unless the user requests a different format, produce:

1. Pre-sermon declaration with the six required statements.
2. Complete sermon manuscript.
3. Concrete weekly response.
4. A formatted `.docx` file that passes the minimum word count threshold.

## Word Document Requirement

- The standard output is a Microsoft Word `.docx` document.
- For a complete sermon, enforce at least 10,000 words unless the user explicitly changes the requirement.
- Build the manuscript first, then convert it to `.docx`.
- Confirm the final word count before treating the job as complete.
