# Paul Scott Wilson Four Pages Method

Use this file as the governing reference when generating sermons with the Four Pages model.

## Core Conviction

The sermon must move through four theological pages:

1. Problem in the Text
2. Problem in the World
3. Grace in the Text
4. Grace in the World

This is not just a structure. It is a theological safeguard that keeps the sermon from ending in law, moralism, or self-salvation. The sermon must always reach grace, and grace must be the final note.

## The OCMAIM Preparation Work

Before writing the sermon, complete the full OCMAIM process:

1. One Text
2. One Theme Sentence
3. One Doctrine
4. One Need
5. One Dominant Image
6. One Mission

For this skill, also declare the listener's existential question as part of the pre-sermon setup because the requested output expects seven declarations before the pages begin.

### One Text

- Choose one complete unit of thought.
- Study its literary, historical, and theological context.
- Ask first: what is wrong in the world of this text?
- Ask second: what is God doing in or behind this text?

### One Theme Sentence

- Keep it short, declarative, and repeatable.
- God must be the grammatical subject.
- Use a strong verb of divine action.
- Avoid conditions such as "if you" or "when you do."

Good pattern:

- "God restores what sin has shattered."

Bad pattern:

- "We need to trust God more."

### One Doctrine

- Name the doctrine that stabilizes the sermon theologically.
- The doctrine should illuminate the sermon, not dominate it as an abstract lecture.

### One Need

- Identify the real human need the theme sentence answers.
- Phrase it as the lived need of the congregation, not as the preacher's abstract concern.

### One Dominant Image

- Choose a concrete visual image connected naturally to the text.
- Use it in all four pages.
- Let it carry different shades of meaning under trouble and grace.

### One Mission

- Invite one concrete action that flows from gratitude for grace.
- Do not make mission a disguised demand.

## The Four Pages

### Page 1: Problem in the Text

- Stay inside the biblical world.
- Narrate the trouble with clarity and emotional reality.
- Do not apply it to today yet.
- Do not solve it early.
- Introduce the dominant image under the light of trouble.

Guiding question:

- What is wrong in the world of this text?

### Page 2: Problem in the World

- Move the camera to contemporary life.
- Show how the same trouble lives now.
- Speak with empathy, not accusation.
- Keep the trouble heavy enough that grace will matter.
- Reuse the dominant image in present experience.

Guiding question:

- How does this same trouble appear in the lives of people today?

### Page 3: Grace in the Text

- Return to the text.
- Identify clearly what God is doing, promising, speaking, or sustaining.
- Keep God as hero in every major sentence.
- Make the connection to Christ organically.
- Use proclamation, not mere lesson language.
- Let the theme sentence ring clearly here.

Guiding question:

- What is God doing in or behind this text?

### Page 4: Grace in the World

- Bring the grace of the text into the hearer's world.
- Show what God is doing now.
- Invite mission as gratitude, not obligation.
- Recast the dominant image in its grace-filled meaning.
- End with divine action, hope, and Gospel confidence.

Guiding question:

- How does this action of God appear in the lives of people now?

## Critical Rules

- God is always the hero.
- The sermon must not end in moral demand.
- Grace is not advice, possibility, or reward. Grace is the present action of God.
- Page 4 must not collapse back into law.
- The theme sentence should appear several times across the sermon, ideally at least four.
- The dominant image should carry memory and theological cohesion across the whole manuscript.

## Suggested Movement

- Introduction: create tension without revealing the answer too quickly.
- Page 1: biblical trouble.
- Explicit transition.
- Page 2: contemporary trouble.
- Explicit transition.
- Page 3: biblical grace.
- Explicit transition.
- Page 4: contemporary grace and grace-shaped mission.

## Final Diagnostic

Before finishing, verify:

- The sermon clearly passes through law, law, grace, grace.
- The hearer ends looking at God, not at the self.
- The mission flows out of received grace.
- The last emotional and theological note is hope rooted in divine action.
