# Timothy Keller Sermon Method

Use this reference when the user wants a sermon built around Timothy Keller's authentic approach, with text-first exposition, apologetic engagement, cultural diagnosis, Christ-centered fulfillment, and grace-based application.

## Core Identity

Keller is an expositor who practices apologetics through preaching.

His audience often includes:

- secular professionals
- skeptics
- artists and intellectuals
- nominal Christians
- committed Christians

His preaching combines:

- exegesis
- philosophy
- literature
- pastoral warmth
- Reformed theology

## Foundational Principle

The text comes first.

Culture is a lens, not the grinder of the lens.

## Exposition of the Text

### What to do

1. situate the text historically and literarily
2. identify the original author's argument
3. surface the surprising or easily missed detail
4. expose the internal tension of the passage

The tone should be analytical, patient, and clear.

## The Two Brothers Framework

For most sermons, diagnose two opposite ways of resisting God:

- younger brother
- elder brother

The younger brother resists through rebellion and self-expression.
The elder brother resists through morality and self-righteousness.

Show how the Gospel confronts both and offers a third way.

## Engaging Culture

Use serious secular thinkers or artists as witnesses to the human condition.

Possible sources:

- C.S. Lewis
- Dostoyevsky
- Kafka
- Nietzsche
- Camus
- Charles Taylor
- Flannery O'Connor

Use them as argument, not as decoration.

## Defeater Beliefs

Name the assumption that keeps the secular listener from even considering the Gospel.

Method:

1. articulate the objection strongly
2. acknowledge what is legitimate in it
3. expose its weakness or incompleteness
4. show how the Gospel answers more deeply

## Indicative to Imperative

Never move straight to moral demand.

Always move in this order:

1. what Christ has done
2. who we are because of that
3. how we now live

Grace, not guilt, drives application.

## Pathways to Christ

Use a specific route from the text to Christ, such as:

- direct prediction
- typology
- unmet law
- covenant faithfulness
- analogy and contrast

Do not force Christ into the sermon without building the argument that makes the move necessary.

## Tone and Delivery

Keller's tone is:

- calm
- layered
- Socratic
- concessive
- serious without being cold

He uses:

- rhetorical questions
- careful concessions
- progressive argument

He does not use:

- hype
- generic motivational language
- guilt-driven calls

## Full Sermon Flow

### Opening

Begin with the universal human question, a cultural observation, or a defeater belief.

### Exposition

Walk through the text carefully and expose the overlooked detail.

### Cultural Diagnosis

Analyze:

1. the secular narrative
2. the religious moralistic narrative

Then address the defeater belief and the two brothers.

### Resolution in Christ

Show why secular and moralistic solutions fail, and why Christ is the only sufficient answer.

### Grace-Based Application

Apply through:

- indicative
- identity
- imperative

### Ending

Offer both:

- an intellectual invitation to the skeptic
- a grace-grounded call to the believer

End with a transformed vision of life under the Gospel.
