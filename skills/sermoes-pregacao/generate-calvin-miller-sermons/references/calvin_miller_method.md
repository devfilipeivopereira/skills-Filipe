# Calvin Miller Narrative Exposition Method

Use this file as the governing reference when generating sermons in Calvin Miller's style.

## Core Conviction

People hear with words but store what they hear in images. Therefore the preacher must think like an artist of images, not only like a manager of propositions.

Calvin Miller's method is not anti-expository. It is narrative exposition: faithful biblical exposition delivered through images, scenes, tension, and resolution.

## The Sermon Logo

Every sermon must have one controlling idea: the sermon logo.

### Requirements

- one sentence
- affirmative
- memorable enough to repeat later
- specific to the text
- urgent enough to carry the whole sermon

Bad:

- "Grace"
- "Faith and obedience"
- "You need to trust God"

Good:

- "Faith begins where control runs out."
- "God enters abandonment before explanations arrive."

## The Narrative Frame

The narrative frame is the central story architecture of the sermon.

### It is not

- a decorative opening story
- a brief illustration that gets abandoned
- a portal to the sermon body

### It is

- a scene that begins in the first minute
- a scene with real unresolved tension
- a structure that stays alive under the sermon
- a scene that resolves only in the closing paragraphs

### A valid narrative frame needs

- sensory detail
- identifiable people
- something at stake
- unresolved tension
- a natural path toward resolution through biblical truth

## The Speech Before the Speech

Before the formal sermon movement, establish relational presence.

- speak conversationally
- shrink the audience mentally to one hearer
- sound like the same person on the platform that you are off the platform

This lowers the ego barrier and signals that the preacher is present for the hearer, not for performance.

## Image-Making

Build exposition through images.

### Strong images have

- sensory specificity
- present-tense immediacy
- a concrete anchoring detail
- a human point of identification

If an image needs explanatory repair afterward, rebuild the image instead of explaining it.

## Planned Redundancy

Return to the sermon logo repeatedly, but each time with a deepened angle.

- first mention: announcement
- second mention: recognition
- third mention: internal participation
- later mentions: inevitability and force

Do not merely repeat the exact same words mechanically.

## Narrative Exposition

Expose the text faithfully, but through scenes, images, and inhabitable moments.

### This means

- stay close to the text
- inhabit the biblical world
- let central images carry the truth
- keep the narrative frame active underneath the exposition
- include confessional honesty from the preacher where fitting

## Application

Application must emerge from the narrative rather than appear as a detached to-do list.

If the frame is built well, the hearer will already recognize personal life inside the sermon before the explicit application section arrives.

## Ending

The frame must resolve in the closing paragraphs.

### Rule

End with an image, never with a proposition.

Do not finish with:

- a list of points
- a summary paragraph
- a final abstraction

Finish with the resolved scene so the final image keeps working after the sermon is over.

## Nine Movement Flow

1. Speech before the speech
2. Opening of the frame
3. Sermon logo announced
4. Narrative exposition of the text
5. Planned redundancy of the logo
6. Returns to the frame
7. Application from within the narrative
8. Resolution of the frame

These movements can overlap, but they must all be present in effect.

## Final Diagnostic

Before finishing, verify:

- there is one sermon logo
- the frame remains alive from beginning to end
- the exposition uses images rather than bare concepts alone
- the preacher speaks confessionally where appropriate
- application emerges naturally
- the sermon ends with a final image
